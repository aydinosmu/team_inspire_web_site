The Project

What is PureFlow?

PureFlow is a water purification initiative that uses waste mussel shells as the base material for a filtration system. The project aims to reduce water pollution while giving a second life to biological waste.

The current focus is improving the material, testing it against different pollutants, and moving from laboratory-scale results toward a real prototype.

Project Focus Areas

  • Dye-polluted water treatment
  • Heavy metal reduction research
  • Waste mussel shell valorization
  • Prototype and certification planning

Our Approach

How the system works

PureFlow uses prepared mussel-shell-based material as an adsorbent. Pollutants attach to the surface and pore structure of the material, helping separate them from water.

1

Collect Waste Shells

Waste mussel shells are collected as a sustainable raw material.

2

Activate Material

The shell-based material is prepared through controlled laboratory processes.

3

Adsorb Pollutants

The material interacts with polluted water and captures target contaminants.

4

Move to Prototype

The next goal is a practical prototype for real-world testing.

Why it matters

Clean water access and industrial wastewater treatment are major global challenges. PureFlow connects circular economy with water purification by transforming a waste material into a useful environmental technology.

Impact Vision

From lab results to real-world deployment

Our long-term vision is to develop a scalable filtration product that can support cleaner water systems, especially in industries where dye and chemical pollution are serious concerns.
