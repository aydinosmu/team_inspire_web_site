The Science & Mechanism

How It Works

Current Stage: Prototype Development

PureFlow uses waste mussel shells as the base material for a sustainable water purification approach. The long-term goal is to develop this material into a scalable filtration prototype that can support cleaner water systems.

Important: PureFlow aims to support the development of cleaner and safer water treatment systems. Potable water use requires further laboratory validation, certification, and regulatory approval.

1. Natural Porous Structure

Mussel shells naturally contain invisible pore structures at very small scales, including micro, macro, and nano-sized pores. These pores have the potential to interact with pollutants.

2. Laboratory Activation

These pores are not fully active in their raw state. In the laboratory, the mussel shells are cleaned, processed, activated, and converted into a fine powder form, making them suitable for water purification research.

3. Contact with Polluted Water

When processed mussel shell powder is added into water contaminated with industrial dyes or heavy metals, and the water is mixed or shaken, the powder particles come into direct contact with the pollutants.

4. Adsorption Mechanism

During this contact, adsorption begins. Dye pigments and metal particles are attracted to the surface and pore structure of the powder. These pollutants enter or attach to the pores and become trapped inside the material.

5. Cleaner Water Output

As pollutants are captured by the activated porous structure and removed from the water, the water becomes significantly cleaner.
